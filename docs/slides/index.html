Decisions Claude made on its own

Things I never asked for

The model list

"Decent cargo space" became 10 models: Sienna, Odyssey, Highlander, Pilot, 4Runner, RAV4, CR-V, Venza, Prius v and Element, each with cargo specs per generation. For Lexus/Acura it skipped the ES/IS/NX/TL for lack of cargo room.

Near misses

Cars up to $500 over budget are kept in their own tab. Sellers negotiate.

Real drive distance

Uses OSRM driving miles and time from McNeil & 183, not straight-line distance. Geocodes and routes are cached in SQLite.

Cross-site de-dupe

The same car on CarMax and Autotrader becomes one card with both links, matched by VIN.

Alert hygiene

At most 8 pushes per run, then a summary. When I changed the price range, it re-ran quietly so I wasn't flooded. "Too good to be true" listings never push.

Trouble flags

"Salvage", "needs transmission" → a Check badge, capped at "good deal". It removed "sold as-is" from the list because "every private sale says it".

Secrets before pushing

Before pushing to a public repo, it moved the ntfy topic into a git-ignored file: "Anyone who knew it could read your alerts."

Reliability source

Chose NHTSA complaints per 10k sold, because Consumer Reports and J.D. Power are paywalled and can't be republished. Airbag (Takata) complaints are excluded.

Report UX

Window-sticker cards, a "Gone" tab for sold cars, Save/Hide stored per browser, and a cargo bar per car.

Self-correction

"117 of 249 cars are great deals." That can't be right.

  • v1 compared every car to a market price learned from dealer listings → private sales all looked cheap.
  • Fix 1: added a dealer-vs-private term, so private cars are compared with private prices.
  • Fix 2: in a fixed budget everything looks cheap, so the labels became relative: great = top 10% of current candidates, good = next 15% (with 15% and 7% floors).
  • Fix 3: a near-new 4Runner for $7,500 is bait, so anything ≥40% under expected price is labeled "Too good to be true?"

Result on that run: 17 great, 31 good, 10 suspect, 19 flagged. Nobody asked it to sanity-check the output.

Round three

Data quality and devops

"Is there any way to scrape Cars.com?"

Search already worked; listing pages hit Cloudflare even in the stealth browser. Claude found that a Chrome-fingerprinted request with the search page as Referer gets through, which unlocks AutoCheck owners, accidents and title. It's cached, capped at 60 pages a run, and stops at the first block.

"Work around the Autotrader blocks"

The block was specific to the browser: plain requests got through. KBB.com is the same company with the same data, so it's the fallback. 116 listings in 55 s, no browser.

"Send errors to a new subscription"

A separate ops topic for failures, recoveries, crashed runs (with traceback) and a run summary. Testing the summary turned up a silent bug: titles with "·" or "✓" had never been sent.

"Look for red flags in Spanish"

Before shipping, it ran the new rules against 937 cached listings. That found 14 "título rebuilt" cars the old rules missed and 20 English false flags ("No check engine light"), plus a bug of its own: $ matching before a trailing newline.

Lessons

What went wrong, and how it got caught

  • Phone layout broke on GitHub. claude.ai had added the viewport tag automatically, and Claude's "phone test" just resized a desktop window, which ignores that tag. Caught by my screenshot. It switched to real device emulation.
  • A drum kit nearly hid 13 cars. FB detail pages include an unrelated feed. Matching "export" would have caught a "Pearl Export" drum kit. Text is now trimmed to the listing itself.
  • Lexus had zero complaints. NHTSA spells it "RX350", not "RX 350". Claude spotted the zero, tried other spellings, and fixed it before I noticed.
  • The claude.ai artifact plan didn't fit: a 255-file limit, external images blocked, and a daily claude -p run. It was swapped for git push to Pages.
  • A crash on the seed run: a flag was read before it was defined. The FB cache meant the rerun took 12 min, not 35.
  • The bot walls move. Autotrader blocked the stealth browser for most runs Oct 4–8, yet plain requests got through. It now tries Autotrader, then KBB.com (same company, same data). Cars.com listing pages needed the search page as Referer. The failure alert caught every outage.

Be honest about this part. Claude's own testing had a blind spot (resizing a desktop window isn't phone emulation), and only a real screenshot from me exposed it. Takeaways

What Claude needed from me, and what it didn't

Only I could do

  • Goals and taste: budget, area, which makes, "Deals first"
  • Judgment calls it framed for me: repo privacy, price floor
  • Interactive auth: ! gh auth login
  • Real-world checks: subscribing on my phone, a phone screenshot, spotting a bad listing

What worked

  • Ask it to ask you questions. Four multiple-choice questions replaced a spec.
  • Short follow-ups beat a long prompt. React to what it builds.
  • Expect pushback. Suggest solutions loosely. It'll tell you when the problem is elsewhere.
  • Give it a way to see results: logs, screenshots, real data. Most of its self-corrections came from looking at its own output.
